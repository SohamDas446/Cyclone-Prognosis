Cyclone Basics

A tropical cyclone is a rotating low-pressure weather system that forms over warm tropical or subtropical ocean waters.

Cyclone intensity can be described using maximum sustained wind speed and minimum central pressure. In general, stronger tropical cyclones have higher maximum sustained winds and lower central pressures.

The Saffir-Simpson Hurricane Wind Scale (SSHS) is a five-category scale used for hurricanes based primarily on maximum sustained wind speed. Category 1 is the lowest hurricane category and Category 5 is the highest.

A cyclone's track describes how its position changes over time. Latitude and longitude are used to represent the cyclone's geographic position.

IBTrACS stands for International Best Track Archive for Climate Stewardship. It is a global archive of tropical cyclone data assembled from multiple meteorological agencies.

The IBTrACS dataset contains observations of tropical cyclones including location, time, wind speed, pressure, storm type, basin, and other characteristics.

Minimum pressure refers to the lowest recorded central pressure associated with the cyclone observations available in the dataset.

Maximum wind refers to the highest recorded wind value available in the cyclone observations used by the system.

Cyclone observations are time-based measurements. A single cyclone can therefore have many observations representing different points along its lifetime and track.
